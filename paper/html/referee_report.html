01

Overall assessment

This is an unusually disciplined manuscript. Its central methodological move — refusing to collapse execution, insight, originality, reliability, and autonomy into a single "autonomy score," and holding every claim to an evidence tier that separates a local measurement from a completed live result — is genuinely its own contribution, independent of the two cases. That discipline is rare and it is the paper's real asset.

The problem is not rigor. It is that the manuscript was drafted in a single sitting on 2026-08-16 and has not been touched since, while the primary case it studies ran for another two weeks and concluded on 2026-09-01. The paper is built as a "living manuscript" with a dated cutoff, a living-outcomes register (§11.2), and a formal post-competition update procedure (Appendix A.6) — all designed for exactly this moment. None of it has been executed. The companion working note was carried through to 09-02; this paper was not.

The consequence is sharper than staleness. The evidence window closes before the most thesis-relevant episodes of the whole project even happened. The AgentSecurity case narrative ends at "late ladders, L31 pending." It never reaches the private-board pivot, the human-designed confused-deputy hedge, the two-submission selection, or the final Silver medal — and that final outcome is the cleanest confirmation of the paper's own argument that the manuscript could possibly have. Fixing the paper is therefore mostly a matter of finishing the study it already designed itself to finish, not re-architecting it.

What already works — protect these in revision

  • The six-capability decomposition (§6.1) and the evidence-tier system (§3.5, A.3) are the transferable core. They survive any outcome update.
  • The governance protocol (§9) is the strongest practitioner-facing artifact — each control is tied to a specific coded failure, not generic "keep a human in the loop" advice.
  • Limitations are first-class and honest (§3.6, §11.1): no human-only control, single coder, N-of-1, hidden evaluators, testimony labeled as testimony. Reviewers reward this.
  • Internal arithmetic is consistent — 14 + 12 + 5 = 31 episodes; statuses (4/10/7/5/1/4) and origins (13/15/3/0) both sum to 31.

Findings register

Blocking3 findings Must be resolved before the paper can be submitted honestly.
B1The living-update was never run§11.2 · App. A.6

The manuscript declares a repository cutoff of 2026-08-16 and reserves post-competition fields "to be populated only after the competitions conclude." The AgentSecurity competition concluded 2026-09-01; the deadline (09-01) is inside the paper's own recorded timeline. Every "unachieved at cutoff" statement (abstract l.40, §4.4 l.507, §5.6, §11.2) now reads as an un-run procedure rather than a genuine open state.

Fix Execute the Appendix A.6 procedure verbatim: add a new dated cutoff (a "cutoff-2" revision), retrieve final official standings, and populate the reserved register fields. Keep the 08-16 states in the revision history exactly as A.6 prescribes — do not overwrite them.
B2The thesis-confirming episodes are entirely absent§4 · §6.2

The paper never once mentions the private board, the CONFUSED_DEPUTY email.send hedge, the two-selection strategy, or the best banked public score (92.670) — confirmed by a zero-hit grep across the manuscript. Yet the final Silver came from the private board via a human-selected hedge, not from the agent cracking public throughput (every attacker-controllable lever failed; the public frontier widened to ~1.59×). This is the paper's "access without autonomy / execution without dependable originality" argument, demonstrated by the actual result.

Fix Add one or two AgentSecurity episodes (private-board hedge design; final two-submission selection) coded as mixed — human strategy, agent execution. Fold the outcome into §4.4/§4.5, §6.2, and §10 as the case's payoff. This is the single highest-value edit in the paper.
B3"Winning objective unachieved" is now incomplete, not wrongAbstract · §4.4 · §11.2

It remains true that the top-prize "win" was not achieved. But a Silver-medal placement (173rd of 4,251) was achieved, on the private board. Stated as a flat "unachieved," the manuscript now understates its own outcome and — more importantly — drops the distinction that carries the argument: placement came from human hedging, the win did not come from agent autonomy.

Fix Report the medal precisely and separate "win / top prize" (unachieved) from "placement" (Silver, private board, human-selected hedge). Attribute it in the paper's own vocabulary of execution vs. originality.
Substantive4 findings Affect rigor, completeness, or peer-review defensibility.
S1The two cases are now asymmetric in freshness§5 · §11.2

ARC-AGI-3 outcomes were never freshly queried (the paper admits this), and the ARC work in fact continued into September (a Flash-Next model-swap campaign, submissions through 09-01+, sessions to 09-04). The register's "No official ARC deadline was admitted" is now likely false, and updating only AgentSecurity would leave a lopsided comparison.

Fix Decide symmetry deliberately: either freshly query ARC's final state, or explicitly bound the ARC case as "observation ended 2026-08-15, not re-queried, competition still ongoing" and say so in the abstract. Do not silently leave one case updated and one frozen.
S2The empirical base is not reviewer-auditable§3.4 · App. A.1

The entire study rests on private agent histories that are "controlled source material" resolvable only by "an authorized auditor." A peer reviewer cannot verify a single coded episode. For an empirical paper this is a real objection, distinct from the honestly-stated N-of-1 limitation.

Fix Release a redacted episode ledger (the 31 coded rows with their public locators — commits, submission refs, section anchors) as supplementary material, and name reviewer-auditability explicitly as a limitation. The evidence ledger already exists; a redacted public cut is low effort and materially strengthens acceptance odds.
S3Single-coder coding, no inter-rater reliability§3.3 · §11.1

Honestly disclosed, but a methods reviewer will press on it because the episode counts and status labels are the paper's quantitative spine. "13 agent-origin, zero human-origin" is a coding result that a second coder might move.

Fix Venue-dependent: either have a second coder re-code a subsample and report agreement (κ), or reframe explicitly as an interpretive single-coder case study and drop any language that reads as a frequency estimate. Pick one before submission.
S4Several outcomes still rest on ERROR rows that later completed§4.2 · Table 1

The L6/L7/L9 vignettes correctly refuse to claim a live effect because the rows were ERROR at cutoff — good discipline. But by the final deadline many of these were re-run to completion. Leaving them as "ERROR, unadjudicated" when completed replacements now exist would be its own kind of staleness.

Fix During the A.6 update, check whether completed re-runs exist for each ERROR-row episode. Where they do, append the completed result (preserving the ERROR state in history); where they don't, keep "unadjudicated" and say it was never re-run.
Precision4 findings Numbers and internal consistency that must be reconciled after the update.
C1The public frontier figure is out of date§4.4

The narrative anchors on a frontier near ~137 (the 08-16 state). By the 09-01 deadline the public top reached 147.530, widening the best-to-top ratio to ≈1.59×. Any gap framing built on the old number understates the finding.

Fix Update the frontier value and the gap ratio wherever referenced; note the frontier method remained unshared/unreproduced in public artifacts through 09-01.
C2The best banked public score is never stated§11.2 register

The register cites the L27 control (88.730) and L29 (85.675) as the "most recent recorded experiment state," which reads as the paper's best. The actual banked best was 92.670. Reporting only the late-ladder numbers misrepresents the case's public ceiling.

Fix State the banked public best explicitly and distinguish "most recent" from "best" in the register.
C3Every count must be recomputed after adding episodesAbstract · §3.3 · §6.1 · §8 · App. A.1

Adding the private-board episodes (B2) changes the totals the paper repeats in five places: 31 episodes, the 14/12/5 case split, the 4/10/7/5/1/4 status distribution, and the 13/15/3/0 origin distribution. A.1 asserts "all 31 episode IDs occur exactly once" — that invariant check must be re-run.

Fix Treat the counts as derived, not typed. After coding new episodes, recompute all six tallies and update every occurrence; re-verify the "each ID exactly once" invariant.
C4The abstract is dense enough to fail a word capAbstract

At ~380 words in one block, front-loading every hedge, it will exceed most venue abstract limits (150–250 words) and buries the lead. The strongest one-line result — a human-hedged Silver while the agent never cracked throughput — is not in it.

Fix Rewrite to the target venue's cap; lead with the confirmed outcome, keep the key caveats, and move the exhaustive hedging into §3.6/§11 where it already lives.
Framing3 findings Positioning choices that shape how the contribution reads.
F1Let the final outcome sharpen the thesisTitle · Abstract · §10

The title makes a general-sounding claim from two cases. The abstract bounds it well, but the "unachieved at cutoff" framing was the weakest possible ending. The human-hedge Silver is a cleaner, more memorable illustration of "access without autonomy" than an open cliffhanger — the paper currently leaves its best evidence on the table.

Fix Rebuild the closing arc around the actual result; keep the title, but make §10 pay it off with the outcome rather than a pending state.
F2Name the target reader§1 · §10

The paper cites the autonomous-lab / robot-scientist literature heavily but speaks to "researchers, IT professionals, research leaders, tool builders." It sits between science-of-science, agentic-coding practice, and HCI, without committing to one. That ambiguity will read as unfocused to a specific venue's reviewers.

Fix Once a venue is chosen, tune §1 and §10 to that community's priors and trim implications aimed at the other audiences.
F3The governance protocol is buried§9

The §9 control table is the most reusable thing in the paper, but it arrives on page ~30 after two long case walk-throughs. Many venues want the transferable contribution surfaced earlier.

Fix Consider promoting a condensed version of the protocol into the introduction or a "contributions" list, with §9 as the full treatment.

03

Decisions only you can make

Three choices materially change the finalization work and cannot be defaulted. They gate the fixes above.

D1 Target venue?

Drives length, abstract cap, blinding (M3), citation style, and whether an N-of-1 interpretive case study is acceptable as-is (S3). Candidates: arXiv preprint · an agents / science-of-science workshop · an HCI/CSCW venue · a "perspectives" journal.

D2 Freshly query ARC-AGI-3, or bound it as unqueried?

Determines whether the two cases are symmetric after the update (S1). Fresh query needs the ArcAGI3 repo + live Kaggle access; bounding it is a one-paragraph honesty statement.

D3 Release a redacted episode ledger?

The largest single lever on peer-review defensibility (S2). Low effort — the ledger exists — but it is a disclosure decision about your own project records.

Recommended path to finalize

  1. Pick the venue (D1). Everything downstream — length, blinding, abstract cap, coding rigor — keys off this.
  2. Run the Appendix A.6 update (B1). New dated cutoff; retrieve final AgentSecurity standing; decide the ARC symmetry question (D2, S1).
  3. Code and integrate the private-board episodes (B2, B3). The payoff edit: add the hedge and selection episodes, then rebuild the closing arc (F1).
  4. Reconcile every number (C1–C3, S4, M4). Recompute all six tallies; re-run the "each ID once" invariant; update frontier, best score, and ERROR-row completions.
  5. Rewrite the abstract (C4) to the venue cap, leading with the confirmed outcome.
  6. Strengthen defensibility (S2, S3, F2, F3). Redacted ledger if D3 is yes; second-coder subsample if the venue needs it; tune the reader and surface the protocol.
  7. Production pass (M1, M2, M3). Re-verify references, decide blinding, regenerate HTML/PDF — last, once content is frozen.
Bottom line

The paper does not need to be re-argued — it needs to be finished. Its own machinery was built to absorb the ending it is now missing, and that ending happens to be its strongest evidence. Run the update it designed, add the two episodes it stopped short of, and reconcile the counts; the rigor is already there.
